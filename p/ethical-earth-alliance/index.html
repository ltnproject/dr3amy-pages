Waste grows faster than most countries can collect, sort and process it. The pressure is highest where consumption is highest.

In plain words: that is roughly 250 kg of waste for every person on Earth, every year. Rich countries produce far more than their share.

02 · Plastic

The problem of plastic waste

Plastic waste is discarded plastic that accumulates in the environment. Global production now exceeds 414 million metric tons a year, and some of it ends up in rivers and oceans.

In plain words: plastic doesn’t rot away. It breaks into smaller and smaller pieces that stay in water, soil and even our food.

Type 1

Single-use plastics

Used briefly, then thrown away.

Example: plastic bags
Type 2

Macroplastics

Large items that persist and entangle wildlife.

Example: discarded fishing nets and gear
Type 3

Microplastics

Tiny fragments and fibres that spread widely.

Example: nylon and polyester

Why it matters

Human health
Plastics can release toxic chemicals into the environment. Microplastics can enter the food chain and may pose serious risks to human health.
Marine life
Plastic in the ocean threatens animals through:
  • Ingestion, mistaking plastic for food
  • Entanglement in nets and debris
  • Habitat destruction
Economy
Costs include cleanup efforts and losses in the tourism and fishing industries.
Policy
Many governments are cutting plastic waste by banning single-use plastics and rewarding recycling.
New · June 2026

Latest research: plastic and nature

The World Economic Forum’s June 2026 report, Plastic Pollution and Biodiversity: a Global Overview, draws on field studies in nine places (Colombia, Costa Rica, Ecuador, Indonesia, Nigeria, Peru, the Philippines, Viet Nam and Maharashtra, India).

The big idea: plastic is not just a waste problem. It harms wildlife, food, fishing, farming and the climate, so fixing it helps nature and the economy together.

130M t

Plastic waste pollutes land, air and water each year

Could more than double to about 280 Mt by 2040, like dumping a garbage truck into nature every second.

10%

Of all plastic ever made has been recycled

Production grew from 2 Mt in 1950 to 460 Mt in 2019. The rest sits in landfills, dumps or nature.

Species affected

From tiny zooplankton to whales, through entanglement and swallowing plastic.

Yearly loss in ocean benefits

One study links 75–150 Mt of ocean plastic to this loss in fishing, tourism and other marine services.

How plastic harms nature

1 · Physical harm
Animals get tangled in nets and bags, or swallow plastic. This affects over 4,000 species.
2 · Damaged habitats
Plastic suffocates mangrove roots, raises disease risk in coral reefs and contaminates sea turtle nesting sites.
3 · Chemical pollution
Chemicals can disrupt reproduction in animals and reduce soil fertility and crop yields.

Risks and opportunities

Business risk

Plastic-related liability cases could reach $100B a year by 2030, with mandatory producer-responsibility schemes adding another $100B. Only 12% of companies have biodiversity targets, versus 78% for climate.

Opportunity

91% of plastic waste is burned, buried or thrown away, wasting $80–120B of packaging value a year. Better design could lift economically recyclable plastic from 22% to 54%.

What works

Cash-for-plastic rewards in the Philippines changed behaviour better than fines. Indigenous communities in Raja Ampat, Indonesia, limit fishing so ecosystems recover.

Source: World Economic Forum, Global Plastic Action Partnership, June 2026. The report notes country studies vary in method, so treat findings as patterns rather than exact universal figures.

03 · Thailand

Thailand’s waste in numbers

Thailand generates over 27 million tonnes of municipal solid waste a year. Plastic pollution is among the highest in Southeast Asia and the national recycling rate is low, so waste management is a priority for sustainable development.

In plain words: of every 100 kg of waste in 2024, about 38 kg was disposed of properly, 39 kg was reused or recovered, and 23 kg was not managed well. Use the tabs to compare years.

What’s new: A draft Sustainable Packaging Management Act went to public comment in February 2025, with enactment targeted for 2027. Challenges named in our slides: rapid urbanization, insufficient recycling infrastructure and limited public awareness.

05 · Solutions

What works

Thailand’s 4Rs framework, plus producer rules, community banks and new technology.

In plain words: the simplest rule is use less, reuse more, then sort what is left so it can be recycled.

Reduce

PCD promotes cutting household waste at the source.

Reuse

Extend product lifecycles by reusing materials.

Recycle

The Green Leaf program supports community recycling networks.

Recover

Waste-to-energy plants recover value from non-recyclables.

Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)
Manufacturers and importers must manage products at end of life. PCD has piloted EPR for packaging, electronics and batteries, pushing companies to fund collection and recycling.
Trash Banks (ธนาคารขยะ)
Villages collect and sell recyclables. Supported by the Ministry of Interior and NGOs, they cut landfill waste and give communities income.
Innovation
Biodegradable plastics, plastic-eating bacteria and circular-economy models. Waste-to-energy and recycling are also key.

07 · Data notes

Check before you cite

Some figures in the source slides conflict with each other or with newer data.

Ocean plastic “19-23 metric tons”
Slide 5 omits “million”. It is also far above the 11 Mt used in the counter. Studies measure different things, so use one source consistently.
Why 11M, 19–23M and 130M tonnes all appear
They measure different things. 11M is the ocean-entry estimate in the counter. 130M (WEF 2026) is all plastic waste that pollutes land, air and water. Compare like with like.
Recycling rate: below 20% or 43%?
English slides say under 20% (19%). A Thai slide says 43%. Definitions of “recycled” differ between sources.
Annual waste and disposal
Slides say 27 and 27.8 million tonnes and 32% properly disposed. PCD reports 25.7 Mt (2022) and 27.2 Mt (2024), with 38% properly disposed in both years.
Unsourced Thai-slide statistics
The slides list 61% compostable organic waste and 78% landfill diversion with no source.
